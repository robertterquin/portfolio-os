The idea

Your portfolio is a little operating system. Each required section is a folder; projects open as apps; your resume is a file on the desktop. A terminal window answers whoami.

Why it fits

  • Fun, very "IT student" in spirit
  • Windows are natural containers for screenshots and certificates
  • Memorable during a class presentation

Watch out for

  • Rubric asks for "easy to navigate": needs a plain list view toggle
  • On phones it becomes a stacked list of windows
  • Most build effort of the four

Inspired by: Maxpanra OS, Mariano Pascual's retro desktop, Bolt's macOS‑style rebuild, MyOS template.

The idea

The whole site is a printed circuit board. You are the microcontroller in the middle; each section is a component wired to you with copper traces. Scrolling powers up the next part of the board.

Why it fits

  • Speaks directly to your IoT project, so the theme has a reason
  • Still a normal scrolling page underneath, which keeps it easy to navigate
  • Real board details (part refs, silkscreen, LEDs) make it feel crafted

Watch out for

  • Dark green everywhere can feel heavy; we'd add lighter "datasheet" pages for long write‑ups
  • Traces simplify on phones

Inspired by: hardware portfolios that split work into PCB, embedded, and web tracks (example README), plus the Awwwards developer award winners that treat one material as the whole interface.

The idea

A hand‑made notebook on graph paper. Certificates are taped in, project photos are polaroids with handwritten captions, competitions are tickets, and section tabs stick out like notebook dividers.

Why it fits

  • Real photos and certificate scans are the decoration, so evidence is front and center
  • Shows personality and growth, which the rubric calls "personal development"
  • Feels the furthest from your minimalist portfolio

Watch out for

  • Needs good photos of your actual work to shine
  • Must keep text readable and typed; handwriting only for captions

Inspired by: Jackie Zhang's scrapbook portfolio, Hattie Stewart and Kennard Lilly (maximalist collage), Studio Feixen.

The idea

Your portfolio is a game character sheet. Certifications are inventory items with rarity, projects are quests with progress, competitions are trophies, and officer roles are your guild.

Why it fits

  • "Current status" for capstone and IoT maps perfectly onto quest progress
  • Empty slots turn future goals into a story
  • Very fun to present to the class

Watch out for

  • Game words must not hide the real info: each item still shows title, issuer, date
  • Pixel fonts only for headings, or it gets hard to read

Inspired by: Bruno Simon (the classic game portfolio), Alex Tade's arcade portfolio in Webflow's maximalism list, Icons8's interactive picks.

My pick: Circuit board, with a dash of the game

It's the only direction whose theme comes from your own work (the IoT project), and it stays a normal scrolling page, so it's easy to grade. I'd borrow the game's quest progress bars for Capstone and IoT status.

What stays the same in any direction

  • All 8 required sections, in your professor's order
  • Title, issuer, and date on every certificate and course, with a "view certificate" link
  • A clear "My role" line on every group project
  • A plain menu so nothing is hidden behind the theme
  • No confidential info: team names only with permission
